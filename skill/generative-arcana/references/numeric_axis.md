# Numeric Axis — Prime / Composite

The fourth axis is not another coordinate. It is intrinsic to the card's number, like spin: the number is already there, and its factorization gives it a character whether or not the author chooses to foreground it.

The axis applies to **both** majors and minors. Major numbers originate on the cards themselves; minor numbers originate on ranks. That storage distinction matters: a major may carry its own authored numeric gloss/visual logic, while a minor inherits any authored numeric interpretation from its rank.

The semantic rule is simple:

- **Identity / precondition** — 0 and 1 are special operators rather than ordinary primes/composites.
- **Prime** — irreducible under multiplication; an energy/condition that cannot be factored into simpler numbered predecessors.
- **Composite** — structurally descended from factors; products combine factor energies, powers stabilize/intensify/exhaust them according to the deck's own reading.

Do not treat arithmetic as decorative symbolism. The number should clarify an already coherent card; if the factor reading strains the card, that strain is information.

## The gloss as signal

A factorization gloss is authored, not mechanically generated. On every major, state what the number's character means **for this card**. Under `majors/primes.md`, composites should close tightly from their factor-majors; under journey/borrowed strategies the gloss may be quieter, but it should still be honest.

If a composite's gloss cannot plausibly follow from its factors, revisit the slot or its factor-majors rather than writing around the mismatch. The failure is a generative diagnostic.

Minors normally keep number latent. Under `ranks/prime_scaffold`, author a rank-level factorization gloss once and let all four suits inherit it. Do not copy it onto every minor card.

## Resonance with the transversal

The numeric pattern and the station cycle are independent periodic structures. Where their patterns reinforce one another, a card can become unusually elemental or unusually tense. A prime-numbered card on a station whose position is itself structurally "prime-like" may feel doubly irreducible; a composite crossing an irreducible station may stage derived structure under indivisible environmental pressure. Use this only where it genuinely sharpens the deck.

## Visual logic

The authored semantic gloss and the visual consequence of factorization are related but distinct. Schema v2 permits `factorization.visual_logic` at the same number-owning layer as the gloss. It states how the number constrains **formal organization** rather than what the number means.

For majors the default authoring profile normally writes this field. Primes/identity can favor an irreducible organizing proposition; composites should inherit formal ancestry from their factor-majors (axes, symmetry, rhythm, nesting, directional systems, scale relations) without literal pasted references or naive object counting. Under `ranks/prime_scaffold`, the same idea may be authored once on the rank and inherited by all minor cards of that rank.

A visual logic that cannot be made to follow from the factor structure is the same kind of signal as a semantic gloss that will not close: revisit the slot or its factor definitions instead of decorating around the mismatch. See `references/visual_language.md`.
